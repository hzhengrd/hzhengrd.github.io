Research profile

Making machines understand
human work

My research sits at the intersection of artificial intelligence and manufacturing — teaching robots to see, interpret and anticipate human activity in real, physical workspaces.

I build multimodal perception, video-based action understanding and embodied AI methods that connect vision-language models with the messy reality of assembly and production. The goal: robots that collaborate with people naturally, transfer knowledge seamlessly, and improve quality and productivity.

Currently I am a Postdoctoral Fellow at The University of Hong Kong (CI3 Lab), working on collaborative embodied AI, human–robot collaboration and digital twins for modular construction with Prof. Xiao Li. Before that, I was a Postdoctoral Fellow at New York University Abu Dhabi, working with Prof. Tuka Alhanai on multimodal vision-language learning and diffusion-based action segmentation. I completed my PhD at The University of Auckland under the supervision of Prof. Xun Xu.

I am always open to collaborations at the boundary of AI and robotics — get in touch.
